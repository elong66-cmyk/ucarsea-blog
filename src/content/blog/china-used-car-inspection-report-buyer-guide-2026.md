The difference between importing from China with confidence and importing on hope is one document: the **inspection report**. Here is how we build ours — and how you should read anyone's, including a competitor's.

## The four sections that decide everything

### 1. Structural / accident status

Cosmetic scratches are noise. What you are reading for is **structural honesty**: chassis rails, pillars, strut towers, weld lines. A proper report grades panels separately from structure and says plainly whether the car has *repaired accident history* or *none*. If a report will not use the word "structure", that is your answer.

### 2. Mileage evidence

An odometer photo proves nothing by itself. Credible mileage rests on **corroboration**:

- 4S dealership service records (China's franchised-service database trail);
- Wear consistency — pedals, wheel, seat bolsters versus the claimed figure;
- Prior listing history where available.

When we publish a figure like *113,000 km* on a 2017 unit, that number has a paper trail behind it, not just a dashboard photo.

### 3. Battery health (EV / hybrid / EREV)

For electrified cars, the battery *is* the price. Insist on:

- **State-of-health percentage** from a diagnostic pull, not a guess;
- Charge-cycle context and any cell-replacement history;
- For range-extended vehicles, the generator's service record too.

No SOH readout, no wire — that rule alone will save you more money than any negotiation trick.

### 4. The photo set

A trustworthy pack shoots the car **the way a skeptic would**: VIN plate matching the documents, engine bay cold, underbody, tyre dates, every corner in flat daylight, interior including the ugly bits. Ten glamour shots and no underbody photo is marketing, not inspection.

## Red flags that end a deal in one line

- Report has no date, or predates the listing by months;
- Seller "cannot" video-call from beside the car;
- VIN partially masked "for privacy";
- Price dramatically below the market curve — in our pricing system, units far *under* benchmark trigger a fraud check, not a celebration. Too cheap is a symptom, and it is usually flood, fire or salvage history wearing a discount.
