Most dealers who add electric two-wheelers to their lineup start with the same instinct: stock the cheapest dirt bike SKU, because dirt bikes are what "electric motorcycle" means in their head. That instinct leaves margin on the table. China's electric motorcycle catalog has a second lane — retro-styled street and café bikes — that moves faster at retail and carries a wider spread between DDP cost and what a buyer will actually pay.

## Two catalogs, two buyers

The utility dirt-bike lane (trail bikes, off-road frames, cargo eBikes) sells on spec sheet: peak wattage, suspension travel, range. Buyers cross-shop on numbers and the deal gets negotiated down to the lowest DDP quote in the market. It is a real business, but it is a commodity business.

The retro lane — café racer styling, whitewall tires, gold-rim limited editions, NFC keyless ignition — sells on look. A buyer choosing a retro-styled commuter is not spec-comparing against three other quotes; they are comparing against what a similar-looking bike costs at a Vespa or Super Soco dealer, which is frequently double or triple the DDP price on a China-sourced equivalent. That gap is the margin a dirt-bike-only lineup never captures.

## What the numbers actually look like

Entry-tier retro units (1000W-class, single battery, styling-forward) land DDP in the $1,300–$1,900 range for Southeast Asia, with UAE landed cost typically 30–40% higher on the same unit due to freight and duty structure. A limited-styling variant — gold trim, custom paint, branded dash — adds a few hundred dollars to DDP cost but supports a retail markup a plain utility frame cannot.

The commonality across every retro SKU worth stocking: **NFC or app-based keyless entry.** It is a small manufacturing cost and a large perceived-value lever — buyers read keyless ignition as a premium-brand signal, not a commodity-bike feature, even when the drivetrain underneath is the same HIMOTO or Freego platform used in the utility line.

## The sourcing mistake dealers make

Dealers who source one electric moto SKU almost always pick a utility dirt bike, because that is the first result on a supplier catalog and the spec sheet is easy to defend to a buyer. The retro lane requires more sourcing judgment — you are buying styling and perceived brand positioning, not just a wattage number — but it is the lane where DDP-to-retail spread is widest.

A lineup with one retro street/café SKU alongside the utility dirt-bike range gives a dealer two price points to walk a customer between, the same ladder tactic used with flagship-vs-entry car trims. Quoting only the utility line leaves that upsell path unbuilt.

## What this means for a first order

- **Do not lead with the cheapest SKU.** A single retro-styled unit in the opening order gives a showroom a second price anchor and a faster-moving impulse SKU.
- **NFC/keyless is worth the small cost premium.** It is the one spec buyers notice without needing it explained.
- **Mixed containers work.** Retro and utility electric motorcycles ship in the same 40HQ as used-car stock — there is no reason to run a separate order cycle for a two-wheeler test line.
