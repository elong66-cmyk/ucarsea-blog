## One number, four parts

A DDP (delivered duty paid) price for an electric motorcycle is four costs added together:

1. Factory price of the unit
2. Freight from the factory to your city
3. Import duty and VAT at your border
4. Clearance and last-mile delivery

The dealer sees one number and pays one number. The supplier carries the customs risk. That is the whole point.

## Why the same bike lands at two prices

The Freego X-MAN X3 on our list is $2,369 delivered in Cambodia or Laos and $2,609 delivered in the UAE. The unit is identical. The gap is freight and duty: overland from Yunnan into Laos and on to Cambodia is short and the duty schedule on two-wheelers is light. The UAE leg is sea freight to Jebel Ali plus a different duty and VAT stack.

Neither number is a markup decision. They are the same margin on top of a different landed cost.

## Comparing DDP against an EXW quote

Dealers used to car imports often ask for EXW so they can arrange freight themselves. For cars that can make sense. For motorcycles at this price point it usually does not:

- A single crated e-moto does not fill a container, so the per-unit freight on a self-arranged shipment is worse than a consolidated one.
- Duty classification on electric two-wheelers varies by customs post. A supplier clearing the same model weekly gets a consistent classification. A first-time importer often does not.
- The time cost of managing a broker on a $2,000 item eats the saving.

The honest comparison is EXW plus your real freight, your real duty and your real broker fee. If that lands below our DDP number, take the EXW quote. On the routes above it rarely does.

## What the DDP number does not include

- Local registration or plates
- Charger adaptors beyond the standard supplied unit
- Retail delivery beyond the address on the invoice

We say this on the quote so there is no surprise at the door.
