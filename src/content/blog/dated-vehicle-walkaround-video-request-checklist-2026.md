A walk-around video is more useful when everyone knows which vehicle it shows and when it was recorded. Give the seller the listing reference first. Ask them to confirm that the video concerns the proposed unit, rather than a similar model or a vehicle used only as an example.

## Agree the recording scope

Request the recording date and a continuous view that connects the vehicle exterior with its cabin. Depending on the buying question, useful areas may include the dashboard, controls, wheels, visible body panels and luggage space. If you need an identification detail checked, agree how it will be matched without exposing unrelated owner information in a public recording.

Ask the person recording to point out visible damage, warning messages or missing items rather than editing around them. Record any area that cannot be shown. Poor lighting or an obstructed view leaves an evidence gap.

## Keep observations and conclusions separate

A seller's video can document what was visible during that recording. It does not by itself verify hidden damage, the accuracy of an odometer, repair history or the condition of components that were not examined. Put those questions into the scope of an appropriately agreed inspection.

## Use the video to refine the next request

Keep the original file or link, date and vehicle reference together. List the points that are clear and those requiring another view, document or inspection. If a different vehicle is later offered, ask for evidence tied to that replacement instead of reusing the previous unit's record.
