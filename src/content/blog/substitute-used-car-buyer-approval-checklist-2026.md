**A substitute vehicle needs its own identity, evidence, cost comparison and buyer decision.** A similar model name does not make the replacement the same purchase. Treat the alternative as a new shortlist item rather than changing the photographs behind an old approval.

This guide concerns a vehicle that becomes unavailable while a sourcing shortlist is being reviewed. It does not replace the written terms for an existing order, an inspection finding, a refund or a contractual dispute.

## Close the unavailable item clearly

Mark the original reference as unavailable or pending reconfirmation, with the date and evidence source. If the supplier has removed a listing, do not continue advertising it as current stock because an old website page or video remains accessible.

Keep its history for comparison. Deleting the old record can make it difficult to understand which unit and price the buyer originally reviewed. At the same time, do not reuse the unavailable unit’s evidence to describe a different car.

## Reapply the hard filters first

Return to the original buying brief: destination, intended use, steering, transmission, fuel, body type, age limits, condition exclusions and budget basis. An alternative that fails a hard filter should not become acceptable merely because it is easy to obtain.

If the buyer is willing to change a hard filter, record that decision explicitly. Do not infer it from silence or from the buyer asking to see more photographs.

## Compare differences side by side

| Check | Original unit | Proposed alternative |
|---|---|---|
| Unit reference and exact trim | Retain original | New reference and source |
| Dates and mileage evidence | Original scope | Review separately |
| Disclosed condition or repair questions | Original notes | New evidence and gaps |
| Price basis and excluded costs | Dated original | Dated alternative |
| Photos and documents | Original only | Same-unit material required |
| Availability or confirmed hold | Last recorded state | Fresh check; no assumed reservation |
| Buyer decision | Original decision | Separate approval needed |

The table should explain meaningful differences, not just display two headline prices. A cheaper invoice may reflect a different trim, mileage evidence or cost scope. A higher number is not automatically justified by calling the alternative “better.”

## Recheck transport and destination questions

A change in vehicle dimensions, powertrain or configuration can affect the questions a logistics provider or destination specialist needs to answer. Reconfirm the actual unit’s transport acceptance, documents and applicable local requirements rather than copying an earlier assessment.

This article provides no country-specific tax rate, loading capacity or admission guarantee. Vehicle-only, origin logistics, freight, insurance and destination costs should remain separate until the applicable scope is confirmed.

## Record what the buyer actually approved

```text
Unavailable original reference and date:
Alternative reference and exact trim:
Hard filters still met / requested exceptions:
Price and cost-scope differences:
New evidence reviewed / outstanding questions:
Buyer decision on this alternative:
Next availability check and agreed transaction stage:
```

A request for more information is not necessarily an order. A prepared quote is not acceptance. Keep the difference between a proposed alternative and an agreed purchase visible in the record.
