## TL;DR

- The **BYD Seal 05 EV** (520km CLTC range) is a compact sedan EV that lands between the Dolphin and the Atto 3 on price, while beating both on range-per-dollar.
- Used 2025 units with ~30,000 km clear **EXW China around $13,000-14,000** — roughly $4,000 below China domestic retail comparables (n=31 benchmark, ~23% under market).
- **Positioning gap it fills**: buyers who want a real 500km+ range but can't stretch to Atto-3/Han money — a segment underserved in Vietnam and Central Asia right now.
- **Central Asia angle**: Kazakhstan's EV import duty exemptions make the math work even better than in Vietnam, where registration fees eat more of the spread.
- Sedan body (not crossover) — narrower buyer fit than the Atto 3, but that narrowness is also the opportunity: less inventory competition per unit sold.

## Why this SKU, and who it is not for

BYD's compact EV lineup has a gap. The Dolphin is cheap but short-range (fine for city-only buyers). The Atto 3 is the volume leader but priced $4,000-6,000 above Seal 05. The Han is a different class entirely — full-size executive money.

The Seal 05 sits in the middle: a proper sedan body, 520km CLTC range (meaning genuine 380-420km real-world), at a price point close to the Dolphin. That combination — sedan comfort, real range, hatchback-adjacent price — doesn't have a clean competitor in most ASEAN or Central Asian used-import lineups yet.

Sell it to:
- Buyers who drive inter-city regularly and were priced out of the Atto 3 or Han.
- Fleet/rideshare operators who need range without paying crossover premiums.
- First-time EV buyers in Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan where duty exemptions make EV economics unusually favorable relative to ICE.

Do not sell it to:
- Buyers who specifically want SUV/crossover ride height — this is a sedan, full stop.
- Markets without home or workplace charging infrastructure — range anxiety complaints will follow.
- Buyers comparing directly against the Atto 3 on badge prestige; the Seal 05 sells on value math, not brand pull.

## Landed cost math (Hanoi example)

2025 Seal 05, 30,000 km, condition C, sourced from a domestic China dealer network:

| Cost line | USD |
|---|---|
| Dealer purchase (China, used) | $11,800 |
| UCarsea margin + inspection + docs | $1,712 |
| **EXW China** | **$13,512** |
| Sea freight Guangzhou → Haiphong | $650 |
| Vietnam CIF | $14,162 |
| Import duty + registration fee + VAT (EV rate) | ~$3,900 |
| Clearance + inland | $480 |
| **Landed Hanoi** | **~$18,540** |

## Landed cost math (Almaty example)

Same vehicle via the China-Kazakhstan rail/road corridor:

| Cost line | USD |
|---|---|
| EXW China | $13,512 |
| Overland freight (Khorgos crossing) | $580 |
| Kazakhstan CIF | $14,092 |
| Import duty (EV exemption applies) | ~$1,100 |
| Clearance + inland | $340 |
| **Landed Almaty** | **~$15,530** |

The gap is stark: Kazakhstan's EV duty exemption saves roughly $2,800 per unit versus Vietnam's EV-preferential-but-still-material duty schedule. For Central Asia dealers, this is one of the cleanest EV margins in the current lineup. Our EXW quote of $13,512 is benchmarked against a 31-listing sample of comparable 2025 Seal 05 units in the China domestic used market, median $17,505.
