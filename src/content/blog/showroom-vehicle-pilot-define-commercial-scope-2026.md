A showroom can be useful evidence of a potential partner’s local presence. It does not, on its own, define who buys a display vehicle, who owns it, or when payment is due.

A first-vehicle pilot should therefore begin with a written commercial brief, not an assumption that “partnership” means the supplier ships a vehicle and waits until it is sold.

## Identify the proposed transaction

Is the proposal a normal purchase for display, a customer-led purchase, a referral arrangement, or a request for consignment or credit? These are different arrangements. A supplier’s willingness to discuss a relationship does not establish acceptance of any particular payment structure.

Do not present a proposed exception as an agreed term. Credit, title, risk, insurance, tax treatment and enforceability may require professional advice and specific approval. This checklist does not offer consignment or financing.

## Define the pilot’s evidence and responsibilities

Record the prospective partner’s business identity, showroom location, intended customers and role in handling enquiries. Identify who would pay for the vehicle, transport and destination charges; who would receive and care for it; and who would handle warranty or service questions.

Agree how enquiries would be attributed and shared without disclosing customer information unnecessarily. A forecast of enquiries is not a guarantee of sales, and a display arrangement is not evidence of an order or profit.

## Consider narrower steps before shipping

Where a physical display purchase is not yet agreed, the parties can discuss a product-information review, a clearly labelled reference display, or a properly scoped introduction to an actual buyer. Each option still requires suitable material permissions and agreement on the responsibilities involved.

No one should claim authorized-dealer status, exclusive territory, supplier-backed warranty or a special payment exception unless those terms have actually been approved.

## Define what would count as success

Specify an evaluation period, the information to collect and the next decision point. Distinguish introductions, genuine buyer conversations, qualified requirements, quotations and paid orders. Counting them separately makes a pilot informative even when it produces no sale.
