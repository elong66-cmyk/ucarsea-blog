Ask a Lagos dealer what changed in the last two years and fuel comes up before anything else. Subsidy reform reset what a working family can afford to run, and the second-hand market has been slow to answer. Buyers still shop for the same badges, then discover the monthly petrol bill does the choosing for them.

That gap is where hybrids sit — and almost nobody is importing them.

## The maths customers actually do

A conventional 1.8-litre sedan in city traffic burns roughly twice what a comparable hybrid does. For a driver covering 2,000 km a month in Lagos or Accra, that difference is not a rounding error — it is a recurring monthly cost that decides whether the car stays on the road.

The objection dealers raise is always the same: *hybrids are expensive, and nobody here can fix them.*

Both are less true than they used to be.

## Why they are no longer expensive

Hybrids are expensive **new**. In China's domestic used market they are not, because the Chinese buyer moved on to full EVs and left hybrid sedans behind. The result is a supply of three-to-five-year-old hybrids priced against ordinary petrol used cars.

Real example from our current stock: **Toyota Levin Hybrid 2022, 50,100 km — $9,525 EXW.** That is a Corolla twin, factory left-hand drive, with the Toyota hybrid system that has been in production for over two decades. Priced like a used petrol sedan, runs at half the fuel cost.

## Why servicing is less scary than it sounds

The Toyota hybrid system is the most-produced hybrid drivetrain in history. In practice:

- The petrol engine is a conventional Toyota engine any competent mechanic can service.
- Brakes last dramatically longer because regenerative braking does most of the work.
- The battery is the component people worry about — and the one that most often outlives the ownership period. Buyers who want certainty can budget for a replacement and still come out ahead on fuel.

This is not a car that needs a dealer network. It needs a mechanic who has read the manual once.

## The dealer opportunity

Right now the West African used market is saturated with the same petrol models from the same auction sources, competing on price alone. A lot with two or three hybrids on it has something the lot down the road does not — and a sales pitch that answers the buyer's actual complaint.

Early movers get to set the price. That window closes once everyone is bringing them.
