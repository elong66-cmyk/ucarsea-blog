## The rule most dealers know

The Philippines does not allow ordinary used passenger cars to be imported for the domestic market. Executive Order 156 has been in force since 2002, and Customs enforces it at the port. A dealer who wires for a five-year-old Corolla and expects to plate it in Quezon City will lose the car.

That rule is why we did not quote the Philippines for most of last year.

## The two lanes that do work

**Subic freeport re-export.** Units can enter the Subic Bay Freeport under bond for re-export to third countries. Subic dealers serve buyers in Papua New Guinea, the Pacific islands and parts of Southeast Asia from that base. For them the question is not "can it be plated here" but "does it clear at the destination". Chinese-market EVs and Japanese-brand units fit that trade well, at EXW prices that leave room after freight.

**Electric vehicles.** EO 12 (2023) zeroed import duty on a list of EV categories through 2028, and the Board of Investments has been steadily widening what qualifies. Brand-new EVs are the clear case. Used EVs are a grey area that depends on the specific customs post and the age of the unit, so confirm with your broker before committing. Where it clears, a two-to-three-year-old BYD or Leapmotor at $12,000 to $16,000 EXW undercuts every new EV on the showroom floor.

## What we will not quote

Petrol or diesel passenger cars for Philippine domestic registration. If a supplier tells you it can be done through a "special permit", ask to see the permit before you pay anything.

## Three checks before you quote a Philippine buyer

1. **Which lane.** Domestic plate, Subic re-export, or EV exemption. The answer changes the unit, the paperwork and the price.
2. **Age and category on the exemption list.** For EVs, confirm the current BOI list and whether used units are accepted at your port this quarter.
3. **Destination clearance for re-export.** For Subic dealers, the paperwork that matters is the third country's, not the Philippines'.
