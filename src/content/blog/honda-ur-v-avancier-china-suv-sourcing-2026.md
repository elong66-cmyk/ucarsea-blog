Most import dealers can quote you a CR-V or an Accord from memory. Almost none have priced a **Honda UR-V** — and that blind spot is exactly where the margin sits in mid-2026.

## What the UR-V actually is

The UR-V is Dongfeng Honda's mid-size SUV, the twin of the Guangqi Honda **Avancier**. It sits **above the CR-V** in Honda's China lineup: longer, wider, and running Honda's familiar **2.0T VTEC Turbo** (the same engine family the Accord and Civic Type R made famous) with all-wheel drive on the top trims.

Because the model was never sold in Japan, Europe or North America, it doesn't appear on the auction radar most importers watch. That keeps demand — and prices — low inside China, while the badge on the nose is still the same H that sells itself in Lagos, Phnom Penh and Manila.

## Who this car fits

- **African dealers (LHD markets):** body-on-frame toughness is not the UR-V's game — it's a comfortable monocoque family SUV. It fits city buyers who want a Honda badge and turbo power without German parts bills.
- **Cambodia:** petrol SUVs clear at normal duty; the UR-V undercuts a same-year CR-V while offering more cabin.
- **Philippines:** left-hand drive from the factory — no conversion questions, ever.

## What to check before you wire money

1. **Mileage honesty.** Six-figure kilometres are normal for 2017 units; what matters is the maintenance trail. We pull 4S service records where available and include them in the inspection pack.
2. **AWD vs FWD trims.** The 370TURBO AWD carries the premium; 240TURBO FWD units come in cheaper but resell slower in SUV-first markets.
3. **Parts pipeline.** Mechanical parts interchange with global Honda stock; UR-V-specific body panels ship from China in 2–3 weeks — quote that honestly to your buyers.
