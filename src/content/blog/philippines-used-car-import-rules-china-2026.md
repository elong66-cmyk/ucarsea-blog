Every week, dealers from Manila to Davao ask us the same question: *"Can you ship used cars to the Philippines?"* Most exporters answer with a shipping quote. The honest answer starts with the law.

## The restriction that shapes everything

Since **Executive Order 156 (2002)**, the Philippines has broadly **prohibited the importation of used motor vehicles**, with narrow exceptions (returning residents, diplomats, certain special vehicles). Courts have upheld the framework, and it remains the operating reality in 2026. Anyone who tells you otherwise — walk away.

What this means in practice:

- **General used-car imports into the customs territory: not permitted.** No exporter can paper over EO 156 with an invoice.
- **Right-hand-drive vehicles: banned outright** under RA 8506 — a separate, older wall that closed the Japan-surplus channel years ago.

## What *is* workable in 2026

1. **Brand-new and current-year vehicles.** The restriction targets *used* vehicles. Chinese-built new cars enter the Philippine market legally — which is exactly why Chinese brands' showrooms have multiplied across Luzon. For dealers, franchise and parallel arrangements around *new* stock are the clean lane.
2. **The freeport question.** Historically, used-vehicle activity concentrated in freeport zones such as **Subic**, under tightly limited conditions that have narrowed over the years. If your business model leans on a freeport, get current written guidance from the zone authority first — rules have shifted repeatedly, and we will not build a quote on top of an assumption.
3. **Left-hand drive supply.** When your paperwork lane is legitimate, China's LHD production is the natural source — no conversion, no RA 8506 exposure, factory steering on the correct side.

## Why we publish this instead of a price list

An exporter who quotes you a "used Vios, landed Manila, no problem" is quoting you a seizure. We would rather send fewer cars to the Philippines and keep every one of them clean. Dealers who work with us get the compliance picture first and the commercial terms second.

If your demand is real but the used-car lane is closed, the conversation usually moves to one of these:

- Current-year Chinese EVs and crossovers as **new** imports;
- Neighbouring LHD markets (Cambodia, Laos, Africa, the Gulf) where our used stock ships freely;
- Parts and accessories supply, which faces none of EO 156's walls.
