Myanmar's used-car trade runs almost entirely on the Japan auction pipeline — right-hand-drive stock landed through Thilawa and re-registered for a market that, unlike most of its neighbors, actually drives on the right side of steering wheel convention would suggest. That mismatch (Myanmar traffic keeps right, most of its cars steer right) is a quirk dealers have priced around for decades. It does not mean the Japan lane is the only lane.

## Why China supply is worth a second look

- **LHD stock, re-registered like everything else.** Myanmar dealers already import and modify RHD Japanese stock to work in a right-hand-traffic market. A car sourced left-hand-drive from China is not a bigger adaptation problem than what the market already handles.
- **EV and hybrid depth Japan's auctions don't have.** China's domestic used market carries EV and hybrid volume years ahead of Japan's auction supply. For dealers watching Myanmar's fuel-cost pressure build, that's inventory the Japan lane simply can't fill yet.
- **A price band Japan-focused dealers haven't compared.** Same-badge, same-year mainstream sedans and SUVs price differently once you compare EXW China against landed Japan-auction cost honestly — not always cheaper, but different enough that ignoring the comparison is leaving margin unexamined.

## What we're actually being asked for

From inquiries routed through our Myanmar-facing channels:

- **Mainstream Japanese-badge sedans built in China** (Corolla, Civic, CR-V-family) — familiar to Myanmar buyers, sourced at China-market pricing;
- **Pickup and light-truck stock** for the commercial and rural-transport segment, where Myanmar demand is steady and Japan-auction supply is thinning;
- **Entry EVs** for dealers testing whether Yangon and Mandalay buyers will move before the fuel-cost pressure forces the question.

## Three things that determine whether the margin holds

1. **Land the comparison, not just the sticker.** An EXW China quote against a Japan-auction hammer price is not a margin until both sides carry freight, duty and prep to the same finish line.
2. **Verify condition before it ships, not after.** Every unit we quote carries an inspection report and full photo set — mileage evidence, panel condition, engine bay — so the car that lands is the car you priced.
3. **Trial small before committing a container.** One or two units across the segments your buyers actually ask for tells you more than any price sheet.

## The bottom line

Myanmar's Japan pipeline isn't going anywhere, and it shouldn't — it's a proven lane. What China adds is a second lane with different vehicles at a different price point, worth running the numbers on before your next order, not instead of your next order.
