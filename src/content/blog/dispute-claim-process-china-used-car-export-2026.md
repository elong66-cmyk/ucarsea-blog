## The question that gets skipped

Most first-order conversations cover EXW price, landed cost, and lead time. Almost none cover what happens if the unit that arrives doesn't match the inspection report the order was placed against. That's a gap worth closing before a deposit moves, not after a container is on the water.

## What a defensible claim process actually requires

**1. A pre-export inspection report tied to the specific VIN, not a generic condition grade.**
If the report references a VIN, mileage, and a dated photo set, a mismatch is provable. If it's a generic "good condition" statement, there's nothing to hold either side to.

**2. A documented condition baseline before loading.**
Photos and video taken at the point of export — not at the point of sourcing weeks earlier — are the baseline a claim gets measured against. Condition can change between sourcing and loading; the baseline has to match the actual unit that ships.

**3. A claim window tied to arrival, not to an arbitrary date.**
A claim process that requires notice "within 7 days of container arrival" is workable. One with no stated window, or a window measured from the invoice date instead of arrival, effectively caps liability at zero for any buyer whose customs clearance runs long.

**4. A remedy that's specified in advance — not negotiated after the fact.**
Partial credit, replacement unit, or cost-sharing on repair — whichever it is, it should be agreed before the order, not improvised after a photo comes in showing a mismatch.

## Questions to ask before the first order

- Is the pre-export inspection tied to VIN + dated photos, or is it a generic condition statement?
- What's the claim window after container arrival, and is it in writing?
- What's the stated remedy for a confirmed mismatch — credit, replacement, or repair cost-share?
- Who absorbs a mismatch found during customs inspection versus one found after the dealer takes possession?

A supplier who answers these before being asked is signaling something about how the rest of the relationship will run. A supplier who treats the question as an insult is signaling something too.
