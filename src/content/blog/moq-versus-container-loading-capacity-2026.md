A catalogue may state how many vehicles fit in a container. That figure describes a proposed packing arrangement. It should not be read as the minimum quantity that a supplier will accept for every model or configuration.

## Ask which quantity the number describes

Minimum order quantity concerns the order the supplier is prepared to accept. Container capacity concerns loading space under a particular packing plan. A sample quantity and a minimum quantity per colour or battery version can be separate conditions.

When reviewing an existing quotation, label each number before requesting more information. If the document already states the model's minimum quantity, reuse that answer. Do not ask the supplier to repeat it merely because the catalogue also shows a full-container figure.

## Compare the same packing configuration

A useful loading estimate identifies the model, packed dimensions, packing method and quantity. Vehicle dimensions alone do not establish how many packed units can be loaded. Accessories, protective packaging and the chosen version may affect the plan.

For a mixed-model proposal, list the quantity of each version. Do not assume that separate catalogue capacity figures can be added together or that a supplier's willingness to supply one sample proves that every mixed order is available.

## Move shipment questions to the actual order

At the product-review stage, keep confirmed facts separate from possible order arrangements. Once the buyer selects models and quantities, obtain the applicable packing and shipment scope. Carrier acceptance, freight and destination charges are then checked for that shipment.

A full-container estimate is not a promise of a shipping price, arrival date or destination clearance. Conversely, a small enquiry should not be rejected solely because a catalogue illustrates full-container packing.
