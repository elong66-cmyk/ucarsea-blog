## The SKU

**Hornet Gold, HIMOTO** — 3000W motor, 60V 45Ah battery, gold-finish rims, smart display, 100-120 km range.

- **MSRP (SEA)**: $2,099
- **UAE retail anchor**: $2,999
- **Spread**: ~30% below UAE retail pricing before dealer/fleet discount

This is not the cheapest unit in the HIMOTO line — the Warrior Classic undercuts it by $400. What the Hornet Gold sells is **differentiation**: gold rims and a limited-styling badge that a stock commuter bike doesn't carry.

## Why "limited styling" is a different margin conversation

Volume commuter SKUs (Warrior Classic, X-11 Street) compete on landed cost per unit — dealers stack them by the container and the margin comes from spread times volume.

Limited-styling variants work differently:

- **Retail price elasticity is lower.** A buyer choosing a gold-rim limited edition over a stock model has already decided price isn't the deciding factor — they're paying for the styling story.
- **Reorder cadence is slower but per-unit margin holds up better.** These move as showroom halo units, not fleet fillers — expect single-digit unit counts per order rather than container-fill quantities.
- **They justify a retail markup a volume SKU can't.** A dealer selling the Hornet Gold at a premium over a Warrior Classic isn't gouging — they're pricing the styling differentiation the same way any limited-edition consumer product does.

## Where this fits in an order

Dealers running a mixed order typically anchor on 1-2 volume SKUs (commuter or adventure line) and add a limited-styling unit as a showroom draw — it pulls foot traffic and cross-sells the volume SKUs sitting next to it.

**Mixed order logic**: volume SKU covers the container-fill economics; the limited-styling unit covers the margin-per-unit upside and gives the showroom something to put in the window.
