## What a range-extender is, in one paragraph

The Li Auto L9 drives as an electric car. The wheels are only ever turned by electric motors. The difference from a Tesla is a small petrol engine under the bonnet that does nothing but generate electricity when the battery runs low. The buyer plugs in at home when it is convenient and fills up at a petrol station when it is not. No charger is ever required to keep driving.

That is why the L9 sells in Almaty, Tashkent and Dubai, three cities where public charging is thin and distances are long. It gives the buyer the quiet, the torque and the cabin of a flagship EV without the question "where do I charge".

## The unit and the number

A 2025 L9 Ultra, the top trim, 6,600 km, essentially new, priced at **$57,198 EXW** out of China. Our benchmark from comparable listings sits at **$50,436**, so this unit is about 13% above reference. We show that rather than hide it.

Why above reference and still worth a look:

**Mileage.** Most L9 units in the benchmark set are 2023 and 2024 cars at 30,000 to 60,000 km. This is a 2025 Ultra at 6,600 km. In the Gulf, where buyers pay for "almost new", that gap is the whole margin.

**Trim.** Ultra adds the larger battery, the rear-seat entertainment screens, the air suspension and the full driver-assist package. Base Pro units make up the low end of the benchmark.

**Six seats, captain's chairs.** For the family buyer in Central Asia and the Gulf, the second-row captain's chairs are the reason they choose the L9 over a seven-seat Highlander or Tang.

## Where it makes money

**Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan.** Li Auto has no official dealer in either country, which means every L9 on the road came through a parallel importer. Demand is real and the retail price is set by what the few units available sell for. A near-new Ultra sits at the top of that range.

**The UAE.** Dubai buyers know the brand from the Chinese EV showrooms that opened on Sheikh Zayed Road. A 2025 Ultra with 6,600 km is a showroom car at a used price.

**Cambodia and Laos.** Listed for both, but the price ceiling is lower. Expect a thinner margin and a longer sale.

## Four checks before a deposit

1. **Battery and range-extender diagnostic.** Even at 6,600 km, ask for the readout. The battery is the expensive part and the easiest to verify.
2. **Software region and language.** The infotainment is Chinese by default. Confirm what the English menu covers before promising a buyer a fully English car.
3. **Charging connector.** China uses GB/T. Your market may need a Type 2 adapter. Confirm what is in the car before loading.
4. **Fuel grade for the range extender.** The 1.5T generator engine wants 92 RON or better. Check against local pump grades.

## Bottom line

The L9 is not a volume car. It is the unit a dealer puts at the front of the showroom to pull in the family buyer who then also looks at the cheaper stock. At $57,198 for a 6,600 km Ultra, this one is priced for the Gulf and Central Asia, honestly above reference for the reasons above.
