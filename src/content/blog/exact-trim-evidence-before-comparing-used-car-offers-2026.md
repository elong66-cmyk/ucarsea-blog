Two listings can share a model name and still describe materially different vehicles. Before deciding that one is cheaper, create a comparison sheet that separates verified identity, seller descriptions and unresolved details.

The purpose is not to collect the longest feature list. It is to make sure the buyer and supplier mean the same configuration before discussing an order.

## Separate the different dates

Record the advertised model year, manufacturing date where supported by evidence, and first-registration date as separate fields. Do not silently substitute one for another. A translated advertisement is a discovery source, not proof of the date shown on the vehicle or its documents.

For destination-specific eligibility, obtain the exact evidence required by the relevant authority or qualified adviser. A comparison sheet cannot establish import eligibility by itself.

## Match the mechanical configuration

Compare engine or propulsion type, transmission, drive layout, seating and any buyer-critical equipment. Retain the supplier’s original version name alongside an English working translation, so that a translation does not accidentally merge two trims.

Where a feature changes the buyer’s decision, request evidence for the particular vehicle rather than relying only on a general model brochure. Pictures of another vehicle or a generic catalogue do not resolve an exact-unit question.

## Keep condition and price scope separate

Mileage should show its source and verification status. Seller photographs and a dashboard reading are not an independent condition assessment. Inspection findings, repair-history evidence and open questions belong in separate fields; blank fields should remain unknown rather than become “no issue.”

Then compare the commercial basis: currency, vehicle-only or wider scope, included charges, validity and any conditions. A difference caused by exclusions should not be described as a saving on an equivalent delivered offer.

## Approve the comparison, not just a model name

Before proceeding, have the buyer confirm the exact version, unacceptable substitutions, budget basis and unresolved checks. If the selected vehicle changes, reopen the comparison rather than reusing approval for a similar-looking unit.
