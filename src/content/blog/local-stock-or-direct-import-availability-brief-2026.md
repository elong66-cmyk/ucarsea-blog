If a buyer needs a vehicle already in their country, a China sourcing proposal is a different offer. Establish which route the buyer can consider before choosing a model, preparing transport costs or promising a delivery date.

## Ask where the actual unit is

For a local-stock offer, ask where the vehicle can be inspected, who has it, and when its availability was checked. A model page, catalog photo or warehouse address does not confirm that a particular unit is there or reserved. If the seller has a specific unit, ask for the identifier and current photos that connect the offer to it.

For a direct-import proposal, identify the candidate unit or exact version, the origin, the receiving location and the quantity. Confirm who will handle each stage. A listing reference can start the discussion, but final availability and delivery arrangements still need an order-level check.

## Compare prices on the same basis

Use separate lines for the vehicle, the origin-side work, freight and destination-side costs. Identify which lines are included, excluded or not yet quoted. A lower vehicle-only number is not evidence of a lower delivered total. If the receiving address or handover scope is unknown, leave the affected cost open.

## Give the buyer a clear choice

Record the buyer's answer as local stock required, direct import acceptable, or route undecided. If local stock is required and you cannot verify a matching local unit, state that limitation. If direct import is acceptable, confirm the exact vehicle or configuration, quantity and receiving location before preparing a specific proposal.

For an electric two-wheeler, also distinguish the intended use from the model name. Confirm the exact version and relevant documents before making any statement about registration or use on public roads.

**A useful first reply:** “Is local stock a requirement, or would you also consider supply from China? If direct import is an option, we can discuss the exact model, quantity and receiving location before preparing a proposal.”
