## Why this car, why now

Most of what we write about in this series is either a pure EV (charging-dependent, harder sell outside Vientiane and Phnom Penh) or a straight ICE car (no fuel-cost story to tell). The Fengyun T9 sits in the gap: it drives like a normal SUV on the highway, runs on battery for city errands, and never strands a buyer who can't find a charger.

Chery's C-DM platform (Chery Super Hybrid) launched in China in 2025 and immediately became one of the higher-volume plug-in hybrid nameplates — which is exactly why the used-market sample size here is 67 comparable sales instead of the 8-15 we normally see on a car this new. A 23% discount to benchmark, on a sample that deep, is not a fluke. It's a car that depreciated the way every first-model-year car does in China's oversupplied domestic market, sitting on a dealer lot in Dalian instead of moving.

## Why PHEV over pure EV for this route

Three reasons we're steering inventory toward LFP plug-in hybrids for Laos/Cambodia specifically:

1. **No charger dependency.** Outside Vientiane and Phnom Penh proper, DC fast-charging coverage is still thin. A PHEV buyer never has to think about it — the car runs on gasoline the moment the battery's empty.
2. **LFP battery chemistry.** No cobalt, more thermal stability, and a degradation curve that's forgiving of the heat and irregular charging habits typical of a first-time hybrid owner. Easier to stand behind without a formal battery warranty program in place.
3. **Resale confidence.** A PHEV depreciates more like a conventional car than a pure EV does — buyers aren't betting on unknown battery replacement costs five years out.
