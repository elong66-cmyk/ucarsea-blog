Morocco has driven on the right since the French protectorate era, which means every car on the road is left-hand drive. That sounds like a footnote until you look at where most used-import supply actually comes from: Japan is RHD by default, and the Gulf re-export route that many North African importers lean on is a mix, with a real share of RHD units that need conversion — expensive, imperfect, and something buyers notice the moment they sit in the car.

China's domestic used market is LHD from the factory floor. There is no conversion step, no conversion cost, and no "converted" stigma to price around. That single fact quietly makes China the structurally easier source for a Moroccan importer, independent of any price argument.

## The price argument still helps

Even setting the LHD point aside, Chinese domestic used values for mainstream badges tend to sit meaningfully under Japanese export-auction pricing for comparable spec and mileage — because the two markets price against different domestic bases. That gap is the margin an importer works with, and it stacks on top of the conversion saving rather than replacing it.

## What actually sells in Casablanca and Tangier

- **Compact sedans and hatchbacks** — Corolla, Sylphy, Civic-class cars for the daily-driver, ride-hailing and small-business segment that dominates urban Moroccan demand.
- **Mid-size SUVs** — RAV4/CR-V class, for buyers trading up from a sedan and willing to pay for ground clearance and cargo room.
- **Diesel and hybrid where available** — fuel cost sensitivity is real in Morocco, and a hybrid powertrain is a genuine selling point rather than a nice-to-have.

Chinese-brand EVs are a smaller but growing lane, mostly in Casablanca and Rabat where charging access is improving fastest. It is not yet the volume category, but it is worth stocking one or two units to test local appetite rather than ignoring it.

## Shipping and paperwork, briefly

Morocco's port entry (Tanger Med or Casablanca) is well used to container vehicle imports, and documentation requirements are standard: MOFCOM export paperwork, inspection report, bill of lading. The friction point is usually age restriction and duty calculation on the Moroccan side, which is worth confirming per shipment rather than assumed static — customs practice shifts more often than the published tariff schedule suggests.
