For decades the Uzbek car market meant one thing: locally-built Chevrolets at fixed prices and long waiting lists. That era is ending, and the gap it leaves is the single biggest opening in Central Asia for dealers who source smart.

## Why Uzbekistan, why now

- **Scale.** 37 million people — the largest population in Central Asia, and the youngest car parc. Demand structurally outruns local production.
- **The duopoly is cracking.** Import liberalisation means Tashkent buyers now compare the default local sedan against imported alternatives — and the price-per-feature gap embarrasses the incumbents.
- **China is next door.** Rail and truck routes through Kazakhstan put Chinese units in Tashkent in days, not the weeks a Gulf or Japanese consignment takes — and without a re-export middleman taking a cut.
- **EV momentum.** Chinese EVs already dominate Uzbekistan's fast-growing electric segment. A used BYD or Aion at 60-70% of new price is the entry ticket most Tashkent families are waiting for.

## What sells

Talk to traders on Sergeli car market and the pattern is consistent:

1. **Hybrid and petrol sedans** — Camry-class prestige, Corolla-class dailies. Chinese-market Toyota and Honda units are LHD from the factory and carry full service histories.
2. **Chinese-brand EVs** — BYD Qin/Song, GAC Aion. Home-market units, one owner, battery reports available.
3. **7-seaters and pickups** for the regions — demand grows the further you get from Tashkent.
