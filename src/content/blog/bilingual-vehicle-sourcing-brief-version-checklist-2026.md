A bilingual sourcing brief should describe one buying requirement in two languages. Start with a short master table and a version date. When a requirement changes, update both versions together rather than sending an isolated correction that is easy to miss.

## Keep the important fields aligned

Record the make, model, trim, acceptable year range, steering position, transmission, quantity and destination. State the currency and what the budget is intended to cover. Where dimensions or electrical specifications matter, include the unit beside the number. Leave unknown fields visibly open.

Model names may be translated, shortened or reused across different markets. Keep the original model or trim designation alongside its translation when possible, and ask for evidence linking the proposed vehicle to that designation. Similar wording is not proof of identical equipment.

## Mark choices and requirements differently

Separate a mandatory requirement from a preference. If you can consider an alternative colour or specification, say so in both versions. A supplier's suggested substitute should be recorded as a new option for review, not silently folded into the original request.

## Close the loop in writing

Ask the recipient to refer to the brief's date or version in their response. Compare the response field by field and highlight any difference, omission or assumption. Resolve a translation conflict explicitly before relying on the offer; do not assume that a later message in one language automatically changes every earlier document.

A brief supports discussion. It is not a stock reservation, formal quotation or order acceptance. Send the current version through the [UCarsea contact page](https://ucarsea.com/contact/) and confirm the scope of any resulting proposal separately.
