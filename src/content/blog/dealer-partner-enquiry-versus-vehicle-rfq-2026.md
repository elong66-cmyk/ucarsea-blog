**A partnership application is the start of a discussion, not confirmation of a purchase or an agency appointment.** The first useful question is what the applicant intends to do: buy vehicles for an existing dealership, introduce buyers, develop a local sourcing relationship or explore a business that has not started yet.

Keep that answer separate from the evidence of a particular vehicle requirement. An enquiry form, company name or public contact channel does not establish purchasing authority, an approved budget or a confirmed order.

## Clarify the role before discussing a vehicle

Ask which market the business serves and how the applicant participates in a purchase. A dealer buying for stock and an intermediary introducing a customer may need different information and different written responsibilities.

Record the stated role as the applicant's description. Do not silently turn it into an independently verified business identity, exclusive territory, authorized dealership or permission to represent another party.

If a fee, introduction arrangement or local representation is proposed, leave it for a specific written discussion. An initial sourcing question does not establish a commission, lead-protection period, exclusivity or guaranteed return.

## Turn a general introduction into a useful buying brief

If the applicant has a current vehicle requirement, collect the information needed to assess it:

| Question | What to record |
|---|---|
| Vehicle | Exact model and version, with alternatives listed separately |
| Quantity | Current intended quantity, rather than an assumed future volume |
| Destination | Country and receiving location, with the importer's role still to confirm |
| Use | Dealer stock, identified customer requirement or another stated purpose |
| Budget | Currency and whether the figure is vehicle-only or includes other costs |
| Decision | Who reviews the shortlist and who can approve a purchase |
| Timing | The applicant's intended review point, without inventing urgency |

Missing information should stay visible. A budget range is not a formal quotation, and a request for photographs is not buyer acceptance.

## Keep business verification proportionate

Use a public business website or an appropriate company contact to understand the stated activity. Record where the information came from and which questions remain open. A website description alone does not prove that a person can bind the company to an order.

Do not request or publish personal identity documents merely to complete a marketing list. If a later transaction requires private verification, agree its purpose, responsible parties and an appropriate channel at that stage.

## Choose the next step that matches the evidence

A general partnership introduction may need a short clarification of the business role. A sufficiently clear purchase requirement may be ready for a shortlist. A particular vehicle still needs its own availability, identity, cost-scope and document checks.

Keep introduction, clarified requirement, proposed shortlist, quotation and agreed purchase as separate records. Progress should follow the evidence rather than the name of a form or a CRM label.
