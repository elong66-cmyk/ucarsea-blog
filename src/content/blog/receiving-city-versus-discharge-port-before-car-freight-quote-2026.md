“Deliver to my city” describes the buyer’s destination, but it does not yet define a transport quotation. A useful request identifies the exact vehicle, the receiving location and where each party’s responsibility starts and ends.

This matters before comparing two offers. One seller may quote a China-side vehicle price, another an ocean leg, and a third a combination that still excludes destination handling or inland delivery. Those figures are not directly comparable merely because they use the same currency.

## Write down the locations separately

Give the supplier your receiving city and country. If you already work with a port, terminal, customs broker or inland carrier, identify that separately. If the route is not chosen, say so; do not invent a discharge port to obtain an apparently complete price.

For a landlocked destination, ask which route and border crossings the quotation actually assumes. A city name alone does not establish an executable route, transit permission or a carrier’s acceptance of that particular vehicle.

## Ask what the quotation includes

Use a short cost-scope table rather than the phrase “all included.” List the vehicle, China-side movements and export handling, main carriage, destination handling, clearance-related services and final inland delivery. Mark each item as included, excluded or awaiting confirmation, with the party responsible.

Ask for the stated trade term and named place where applicable. A trade-term label is not a substitute for reading the quotation, transport conditions and written allocation of responsibilities. Confirm any unclear term with the appropriate logistics or customs professional.

## Check the exact vehicle before booking

Vehicle dimensions, operating condition, fuel or battery configuration, documents and intended destination can affect the carrier’s acceptance and quotation. Do not treat a price for a different model or an earlier route as a current shipping commitment.

For a useful first enquiry, send the destination country and city, exact model/version, quantity, intended use, proposed timing and any preferred receiving agent. Ask which facts remain unverified and who will verify them.
