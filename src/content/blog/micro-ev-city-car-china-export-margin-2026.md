Every dealer conversation about China EVs starts at the same altitude: BYD Atto 3, Geely Geometry C, Wuling Bingo. Real cars, real margin, well-covered ground. One tier below that — the micro-EV city car, priced closer to a used motorcycle than a used SUV — barely comes up. That is the gap.

That gap is wider than most mid-tier EV comparisons this quarter, on a car that costs less than half what a Bingo or Atto 3 runs EXW. It didn't get there because the car is bad — it got there because almost nobody outside China is actively sourcing this segment, so the domestic resale curve hasn't been arbitraged by export demand yet.

## Why this tier is underpriced relative to demand

- **It's not a "real car" to most export buyers.** Two-door, sub-4-meter, city-only range — dealers who think in SUV and sedan categories skip past it without pricing it.
- **China's domestic market treats it as a commuter appliance**, which means depreciation is steep and fast — good for the buyer on the other end of that curve.
- **Battery packs are small and cheap to replace**, which lowers the risk ceiling compared to a mid-size EV with a $4,000+ pack.
- **Charging-light markets can still use it.** A 100–150 km range city EV doesn't need a national charging network — it needs one outlet at the dealer lot and a buyer who drives inside a capital city.

## Where it actually sells

This is not a rural or highway car, and pretending otherwise is how a good unit becomes a bad listing. The buyer profile is narrow and specific:

- **Urban ride-hail and delivery fleets** in capital cities — Lagos, Addis Ababa, Erbil, Dubai's cheaper zones — where daily range is predictable and charging happens at a depot, not roadside.
- **Second-car households** in fuel-import-constrained markets, where a short-range EV is a rational trade against fuel cost, not a compromise.
- **Driving schools and campus/compound fleets**, where the car never leaves a bounded area.

If a dealer's market doesn't have at least one of those three buyer types, this tier isn't worth stocking yet — that's the honest caveat, not a sales pitch.

## The margin case

At $8,716 EXW plus freight, a micro-EV lands well under $10,000 in most markets this size ships to. That price point competes directly with **used fuel-powered hatchbacks that need scheduled maintenance the EV doesn't** — oil changes, timing components, exhaust systems, all zero on an EV drivetrain. For a fleet buyer running 15–30 units, that operating cost delta compounds fast, and it's a stronger pitch than range or top speed.

## The catch

- **Resale ceiling is real.** This is not a car that holds value like a mid-size EV — buy it to move fast, not to bank equity.
- **Parts availability outside major cities is thin.** This is a metro-market car; don't ship it to a dealer whose lot is three hours from the nearest charging point or Baojun-compatible service shop.
- **One unit does not make a category.** We are flagging this as an underpriced tier worth testing with two or three units, not declaring it a proven high-volume line yet.

## Dealer playbook

1. **Test with 2–3 units in one metro market** before committing to a container-scale order.
2. **Pre-qualify the buyer as fleet or urban-only** — this car will disappoint a buyer expecting highway range.
3. **Price the maintenance-cost story, not the speed story.** That's the argument that actually lands with fleet operators.

## The bottom line

The micro-EV shelf is priced like nobody wants it, because almost nobody outside China is looking at it yet. For the right metro-fleet buyer, that mispricing is the opportunity.
