Unlike the domestic-only nameplates we usually write about — the INSPIRE, the Wuling EVs — the Civic needs no explanation. Every dealer in Vientiane or Phnom Penh already knows the badge, already has buyers lined up, and already knows what a clean 10th-generation Civic sells for. That familiarity is exactly why the sourcing gap on this one is easy to miss: everyone assumes a car this well-known is priced the same everywhere.

It is not.

## Why the gap exists on a car everyone already knows

The Civic is a global nameplate, but China's domestic market runs its own pricing cycle — driven by local resale volume, not what the same generation fetches in a Japan-spec export channel. A well-known badge does not automatically mean synced pricing across markets. It just means dealers rarely check, because "everyone already knows the Civic" feels like there is nothing left to find.

That is the actual arbitrage here: not an obscure model, just an under-checked price line on a car with obvious resale demand.
